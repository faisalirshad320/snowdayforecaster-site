School closing predictions, and how far to trust them

What a prediction can and cannot tell you the night before — and how to judge any predictor, including this one.

A school closing prediction is a probability that your district cancels classes, worked out from the weather forecast and from how districts like yours have behaved before. It is not an announcement, and no prediction can be certain the night before.

This site's predictions were back-tested on 1,345 school days in 20 large US districts, with every miss and false alarm published. Enter a ZIP code on the calculator for your own number.

Prediction is not the same as a closure list

Two different things get searched for with the same words. A closure list is a feed of announcements a district has already made; your local TV station and your district's app are the fastest sources for that. A prediction is a probability for a decision nobody has made yet. This site does the second. It will never tell you a school is closed, because it does not receive announcements.

Four tests for any closing predictor

  1. Does it publish a calibration table? Not a single "97% accurate" claim — a table showing what actually happened at each forecast level. Ours is below.
  2. Was it tested where it had not been trained? A model scored on districts it learned from will always look good. We scored each district with a model trained only on the other 19.
  3. Does it list its misses? Every model has them. Hiding them is the tell.
  4. Does it account for ice and cold, not just snow depth? Depth-only rules miss most real closures.

What districts actually weigh

  • Road and sidewalk condition at 4am, especially side streets and hilly bus routes.
  • Ice — freezing rain and sleet close schools at amounts that barely register as snowfall.
  • Wind chill for children waiting at bus stops, which is why cold days exist at all.
  • Timing. The same storm landing at 6am rather than 6pm changes everything.
  • Calendar pressure. Districts that have used up their built-in snow days increasingly call a remote day instead.

Questions

How accurate are school closing predictions?

Ours were tested on 1,345 school days across 20 districts, scoring each district with a model that had never seen it. Brier score 0.033, AUC 0.95, and 76% of calls above 50% were right. Any predictor that will not show you a calibration table is asking you to take accuracy on faith.

Do you list which schools are closed?

No. We forecast the chance of a closure; we do not carry a live closure list. For the actual list, use your district's website or app and your local TV station, which receive the announcements directly.

Can a prediction be wrong?

Often, and knowably so. At a 50% cut-off our model missed 46 of 83 closure days and raised 12 false alarms across 1,262 normal days. Those misses are listed on the methodology page rather than hidden.

Why did school close when barely any snow fell?

That is the normal case, not the exception: 64% of closures we tracked followed less than an inch of new snow. Ice on untreated roads, leftover pack and extreme cold close far more schools than fresh depth does.
