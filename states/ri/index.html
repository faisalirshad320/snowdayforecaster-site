How much snow closes school in Rhode Island?

In Rhode Island it takes roughly 5.2 inches of new snow for the chance of a school closing to reach 50%, and about 9 inches for 80%, for a plain overnight snowfall with no ice and no snow already on the ground. Cold alone starts closing Rhode Island schools around -16°F wind chill.

Ice and leftover snowpack move those numbers sharply: 64% of the closures we tracked in 2025-26 followed less than an inch of new snow in the previous 48 hours.

When Rhode Island snow days happen

Most closures fall between late December and the end of February, driven by nor'easters; a late-March storm catches districts that have already used their spare days.

Where Rhode Island school closings are announced

Closures are decided district by district. The largest districts in Rhode Island by enrollment, whose calls tend to set the tone for their neighbours:

  • Cranston Public Schools

Official announcements come from the district itself — its website, app, text and email alerts — and are mirrored on local closings lists. In Rhode Island the lists most families check:

  • WPRI and WJAR closings lists
  • Rhode Island Broadcasters Association
  • district alerts

This site forecasts the chance of a closure; it does not list closures. For whether your school is actually closed, use the district's own channel.

Make-up days and remote days in Rhode Island

Rhode Island requires 180 days. Since 2021 the state lets districts count virtual learning days, so a storm often becomes a remote day rather than a cancellation — but delays remain the most common call on marginal mornings.

Rules change and districts apply them differently; treat this as background, not policy.

How much snow does it take to cancel school in Rhode Island?

About 5.2 inches of new snow for a 50% chance and 9 inches for 80%, for a plain overnight snowfall. Freezing rain closes Rhode Island schools at almost any depth, and wind chill around -16°F can close them with no snow at all.
