How much snow closes school in New Mexico?

In New Mexico it takes roughly 1 inch of new snow for the chance of a school closing to reach 50%, and about 1.5 inches for 80%, for a plain overnight snowfall with no ice and no snow already on the ground. Cold alone starts closing New Mexico schools around -15°F wind chill.

Ice and leftover snowpack move those numbers sharply: 64% of the closures we tracked in 2025-26 followed less than an inch of new snow in the previous 48 hours.

When New Mexico snow days happen

Most winters bring one or two closure days, usually in January, and some winters none at all. Ice, not snow, is the usual reason.

Where New Mexico school closings are announced

Closures are decided district by district. The largest districts in New Mexico by enrollment, whose calls tend to set the tone for their neighbours:

  • Albuquerque Public Schools
  • Las Cruces Public Schools
  • Rio Rancho Public Schools

Official announcements come from the district itself — its website, app, text and email alerts — and are mirrored on local closings lists. In New Mexico the lists most families check:

  • KOB, KRQE and KOAT (Albuquerque)
  • district alerts

This site forecasts the chance of a closure; it does not list closures. For whether your school is actually closed, use the district's own channel.

Make-up days and remote days in New Mexico

New Mexico requires 180 days and expects closures to be made up, though Albuquerque Public Schools more often calls a two-hour delay than a closure. Ice and an overnight storm on the morning commute close Albuquerque; Santa Fe and Taos run on much more snow.

Rules change and districts apply them differently; treat this as background, not policy.

How much snow does it take to cancel school in New Mexico?

About 1 inch of new snow for a 50% chance and 1.5 inches for 80%, for a plain overnight snowfall. Freezing rain closes New Mexico schools at almost any depth, and wind chill around -15°F can close them with no snow at all.
