Snow day chances in Colorado

How much snow it takes to close school in Colorado, and why the number is not the same as the state next door.

In Colorado it takes roughly 6.2 inches of new snow for the chance of a school closing to reach 50%, and about 11.1 inches for 80%, for a plain overnight snowfall with no ice and no snow already on the ground.

Cold alone, with no snow at all, starts closing Colorado schools around -35°F wind chill. Colorado sits in the Northern snow belt region of this model. For tomorrow's actual number, enter your ZIP code.

What closes schools in the Northern snow belt

Districts here run big plough fleets and expect snow all winter, so it takes a lot to close them. When they do close it is usually for blizzard-force wind, ice, or wind chill deep below zero rather than snow depth alone.

Two things matter more than the headline snow total almost everywhere, Colorado included. The first is ice: freezing rain and sleet barely register as snow depth but close schools at once. The second is what is already on the ground — 64% of the closures in our dataset followed less than an inch of new snow in the previous 48 hours, because the storm had passed and the roads had not.
