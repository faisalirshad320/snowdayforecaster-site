How much snow closes school in Massachusetts?

In Massachusetts it takes roughly 5.2 inches of new snow for the chance of a school closing to reach 50%, and about 9 inches for 80%, for a plain overnight snowfall with no ice and no snow already on the ground. Cold alone starts closing Massachusetts schools around -16°F wind chill.

Ice and leftover snowpack move those numbers sharply: 64% of the closures we tracked in 2025-26 followed less than an inch of new snow in the previous 48 hours.

When Massachusetts snow days happen

Most closures fall between late December and the end of February, driven by nor'easters; a late-March storm catches districts that have already used their spare days.

Where Massachusetts school closings are announced

Closures are decided district by district. The largest districts in Massachusetts by enrollment, whose calls tend to set the tone for their neighbours:

  • Boston Public Schools
  • Springfield Public Schools
  • Worcester Public Schools
  • Brockton Public Schools
  • Lynn Public Schools

Official announcements come from the district itself — its website, app, text and email alerts — and are mirrored on local closings lists. In Massachusetts the lists most families check:

  • WCVB, WBZ and NBC10 Boston closings lists
  • WWLP (Springfield)
  • Boston.com and MassLive closings pages
  • district robocalls

This site forecasts the chance of a closure; it does not list closures. For whether your school is actually closed, use the district's own channel.

Make-up days and remote days in Massachusetts

Massachusetts requires 180 days and asks districts to schedule 185. The state does not count remote learning toward the 180, so Massachusetts districts close outright for a storm and make the day up in June rather than going virtual.

Rules change and districts apply them differently; treat this as background, not policy.

How much snow does it take to cancel school in Massachusetts?

About 5.2 inches of new snow for a 50% chance and 9 inches for 80%, for a plain overnight snowfall. Freezing rain closes Massachusetts schools at almost any depth, and wind chill around -16°F can close them with no snow at all.
