Snow Day Calculator

The chance your school closes tomorrow, from the newest forecast for your ZIP code. Free, no sign-up, and tested against real 2025-26 closures.

A snow day calculator estimates the chance your school closes tomorrow. Enter a US ZIP code and this one returns a percentage for the next three school days, using the current forecast for your grid point and a model trained on how 20 large districts actually decided in winter 2025-26.

It is free and needs no sign-up. On the 1,345 school days we tested, its Brier score was 0.033 — 42% better than guessing the average — and when it said 50-70%, schools closed or went remote 62% of the time.

What actually closes schools

64% of the closure days we tracked in 2025-26 had less than an inch of new snow in the 48 hours before.

Calculators that only count inches miss most snow days. The model weighs four things, in this order of how often they mattered:

  • Snow and ice still on the ground. 44 of 83 closure days came after the storm had ended, while roads stayed frozen.
  • New frozen precipitation. Snow, sleet and freezing rain in the last 48 hours, measured as water, so a quarter inch of ice counts.
  • Wind chill. Chicago and Milwaukee closed on January 23, 2026 with almost no snow at wind chills near −20°F.
  • Where you live. Southern districts close for amounts that northern districts ignore, and the model knows the difference.

Questions

What are the chances of a snow day tomorrow?

Enter your ZIP code above. The calculator reads the latest forecast for your area and returns a percentage for tomorrow and the next two days, based on how 20 large US districts actually decided in winter 2025-26.

How accurate is the snow day calculator?

We tested it on 1,345 school days in 20 districts. When it said 50-70%, schools closed or went remote 62% of the time. Its Brier score was 0.033, 42% better than always guessing the average. See the full back-test on the methodology page.

Why does it say my chance is high when little snow is forecast?

Because that is how districts behave. 64% of the 2025-26 closure days we tracked had less than 1 inch of new snow in the previous 48 hours. Most were the days after a storm, when ice and packed snow were still on the roads, or days of freezing rain or extreme cold.

Does it work for my school district?

It works for any US ZIP code. It adjusts for how used your region is to snow: the same inch of ice closes schools in Atlanta or Dallas but not in Buffalo. It does not yet know your district's own policy, so treat the result as a regional estimate.

What about two-hour delays?

Delays are not modeled separately yet. In our back-test, delay days scored between normal days and closures, so a result of roughly 12-45% is labeled “Delay possible”.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

Yes, it is free, and there is no sign-up. Your ZIP code is stored only in your own browser so the page remembers it.
