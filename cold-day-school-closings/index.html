Cold days: when schools close for wind chill alone

No snow, no ice, and school is still cancelled. Here is the number districts are watching.

Schools close for cold based on wind chill, not air temperature, because the risk is frostbite at the bus stop. There is no national threshold — policies range from about −20°F to below −30°F wind chill, and southern districts act much earlier than northern ones.

Running this site's model forward for cold alone, with no snow at all, the wind chill at which the odds pass 50% is about -35°F in the northern snow belt and -15°F in the South.

Read these as regional averages, not as any one district's policy. The spread within a region is wide — that is the honest limit of a model that does not yet know your district's own rule.

What January 2026 showed

The cold snap of 23 January 2026 is the cleanest example in our data. Chicago Public Schools closed with almost no snow on the ground, at wind chills near −20°F. Minneapolis, 400 miles north and colder still at around −31°F, held classes. Same weather event, opposite decisions, entirely because of what each district is built and staffed for.

This is why a pure weather threshold cannot work nationally, and why the model carries a regional term instead of one universal cut-off.

What a district weighs on a cold morning

  • Wind chill at 6-8am, when children are outside waiting, not the afternoon high.
  • How many students walk or wait for buses versus being driven.
  • Whether buses will start. Diesel fleets struggle in deep cold, and a district that cannot run half its routes cannot open.
  • Building heat. Older buildings lose heating capacity in extreme cold, which closes individual schools even when the district stays open.

Questions

How cold does it have to be for school to close?

There is no national rule. Wind chill, not air temperature, is the number districts use, and thresholds vary enormously: Chicago closed near −20°F wind chill in January 2026 while Minneapolis stayed open at −31°F. Many districts publish a threshold in the −20°F to −30°F wind chill range; southern districts act far earlier.

Why wind chill and not temperature?

Because the risk is frostbite on a child waiting at a bus stop or walking to school, and that depends on how fast exposed skin loses heat — which wind chill measures and plain temperature does not.

Do cold days count as snow days?

Administratively, usually yes: they come out of the same bank of emergency days. Increasingly districts call a remote day instead so the calendar is not extended.

Does the calculator handle cold-only closures?

Yes. Wind chill is one of its inputs, and the regional interaction means the same wind chill counts differently north and south. It is the input the model is least certain about, because district cold policies differ more than their snow policies.
